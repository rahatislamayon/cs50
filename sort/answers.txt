sort1 uses: bubble sort

How do you know?: It was slow on random data but sped up a lot on already-sorted input, which happens because bubble sort stops early once no swaps are needed.

sort2 uses: merge sort

How do you know?: It scales far better than the other two and is consistently very fast, which is characteristic of O(n log n).

sort3 uses:  selection sort

How do you know?: Runtime doesn’t change between random and sorted input, which is the hallmark of selection sort’s fixed O(n^2) comparisons.
